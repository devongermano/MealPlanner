# Dish review — your checklist

**What this is.** When your recipes were loaded into the system, each one got taken
apart into the things you actually batch-cook: a pot of picadillo, a stack of gordita
shells, a jar of salsa. That part was right. What got thrown away was the other half of
every recipe — *how the parts go back together on a plate.* That is why a day came out
looking like a pile of unrelated food.

This document is the reconstruction of that missing half. Sixteen dishes, each one a
named plate with the amounts that make it that plate. Some come straight from the
recipes you gave; some are a cook's best guess at a format; three are outright
inventions that exist to give a lonely component somewhere to live. Each dish says
which it is, in plain words, before you read a single number.

**Every gram in here is provisional.** They are reconstructions, not measurements. The
point of this document is for you to fix them.

**How to work through it.** One dish per section. Read the one-line description, look at
the amounts, then tick one box:

- **approve** — this is the dish, and those amounts are about right.
- **edit** — right dish, wrong numbers (or wrong parts). Write what it should be.
- **reject** — this isn't a real dish, or we'd never eat it. Say so and it's gone.

The **baseline** is one normal serving — the shape of the dish. The range next to it is
how far the amounts may move for a bigger or smaller eater before the plate stops
reading as that dish. So: what's the smallest serving of this you'd still call a real
one, and the biggest you'd put in front of someone without embarrassment?

**Optional** items are per-person finishing. That's the whole reason salsa and cheese
are separate recipes: one pot stays universal until it hits the plate, so Devon's
burrito gets zero cheddar and Jimbo's gets thirty grams — without cooking two breakfasts.

Macros shown for each dish are one baseline serving *with* the optional items, derived
from the ingredients (never typed in by hand). Rough reference points: a third of
Devon's day is about 60 g protein / 32 g fat / 107 g carb (≈950 kcal); a third of
Jimbo's is about 78 g / 52 g / 196 g (≈1,570 kcal).

There are seven general questions at the end, after the dishes. The last two are
quick label checks from a separate pass over the ingredient data — they're here so
everything you owe an answer on lives in one document.

can't add protein, so one of those is a brand difference and one is a guess. This is

sugo, so being wrong here moves the sauce's protein by about 12%.
